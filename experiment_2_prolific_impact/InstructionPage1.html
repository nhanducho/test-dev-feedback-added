Experiment Description

In this experiment, you will take the role of an executive manager of a company.

Your company operates in a global supply chain that is exposed to the risk of supply chain disruptions. A disruption will lead to substantial losses, for example, because of unfulfilled orders, operational interruptions, and damage to the company’s reputation.

To reduce this risk, you are advised to build supply chain resilience. Supply chain resilience is a firm’s ability to respond to and recover from a disruption, thereby reducing the loss caused by it.

In each round of the experiment, your task is to decide how much money to allocate to supply chain resilience to reduce the loss your company would suffer if a disruption occurs.

Detailed information and the procedure for the experiment are explained below and on the following pages.

Experimental Procedure

You will play a decision-making game consisting of 100 rounds. In each round, you will complete the same decision task.

At the beginning of each round, your company always earns 100 ECU in gross profit. ECU stands for Experimental Currency Unit.

You then decide how much of this 100 ECU to spend on supply chain resilience. You can freely choose any amount between 0 and 100 ECU.

The results in each round will be calculated and displayed in the performance table. The table shows the gross profit, resilience spending, disruption loss (if a disruption occurred), and net profit for each round.

○ If no disruption occurs, your net profit for the round is:
Net Profit = Gross Profit – Resilience Spending

○ If a disruption occurs, your net profit for the round is:
Net Profit = Gross Profit – Resilience Spending – Disruption Loss

Your net profit may be positive or negative. If your net profit is negative in a round, it will reduce your cumulative profit up to that round.
