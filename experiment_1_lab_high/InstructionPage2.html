Supply Chain Disruptions

Your corporation is constantly at risk of being affected by supply chain disruptions (e.g., a flood in your factory, shipping route blockage, or political unrest). Suppose your business is disrupted or indirectly affected by adverse events (e.g., your suppliers are shut down). In that case, you may fail to meet your customers' demands and consequently incur substantial costs due to declining sales and losing customers to your competitors. For example, imagine your company has signed deals with various customers to supply them with products you produce, and these deals bring you 100 ECU of gross profit once all deals are fulfilled. Since you believe there will be no disruption in this round, you decide not to spend anything on SC resilience (0 ECU). It turns out that you are right, which helps your company earn the largest possible net profit of 100 ECU. Alternatively, your speculation proves to be incorrect, and your business is disrupted. In this case, you cannot fulfill your customer obligations due to a zero-inventory buffer, "just-in-time" manufacturing, and consolidated procurement, resulting in the most significant costs for your company related to SC disruptions.

Disruptions are unpredictable, but you do know that without SC resilience (zero spending), the probability of a disruption occurring is 20% and it costs you 500 ECU once a disruption occurs. Keep in mind that the disruption occurring (or not) in one round is independent and does not influence the result of any other rounds.

How Supply Chain Resilience Spending Works

SC resilience is widely regarded as one of the best strategies that enables your company to proactively prepare for unforeseen disruptions. In this experiment, spending on SC resilience allows you to reduce both the probability and the financial impact of possible disruptions. The rule is simple: the higher you spend on SC resilience, the lower the likelihood of disruptions occurring, as well as the lower the disruption impacts if disruptions do happen. For example, if you decide to spend 20 ECU on SC resilience, the new probability of disruption occurring is now 16%, and the new impact in the event of disruption is 400 ECU. You can also choose not to spend on SC resilience by inserting 0 ECU, which means that you accept the disruption probability of 20% and the potential loss of 500 ECU. Conversely, you can decide to spend up to a maximum of 100 ECU to eliminate all risks of SC disruptions (i.e., 0% disruption probability, 0 ECU of impact). The revised probability and revised impact of disruptions corresponding to the particular amount of spending are shown in the table below. During the experiment, a decision support tool is always available to help you calculate the exact revised probability and revised impact of disruptions based on your specific spending amount.

Your Objective

Your goal in this experiment is to maximize your profit by deciding the right amount to spend on SC resilience that serves you best in preventing SC disruptions, while not eating up your profit due to excessive spending. Please note that your final profit will be converted to your actual bonus and added to your show-up fee.
